The Naro field guide

Small guides.
A smoother start.

A few practical steps to feel at home with Naro.

For Naro on macOS 26+ and Apple Silicon. Available from GitHub Releases.

Getting started

Your first file,
from start to finish.

A double-click is all you need once Naro is connected.

  1. Open Naro and connect Google.

    Start Naro from your Applications folder. Choose the Google sign-in button. Your browser will open so you can select an account and grant the requested access. Return to Naro when sign-in finishes.

  2. Make Naro your default.

    Open the account screen using the account icon in the toolbar. Find Opening from Finder and choose Configure…, then confirm the system’s default-application selection.

    Choose your language in the Naro menu → Language. Use “Follow macOS” to match your system language.

  3. Double-click an Office document.

    In Finder, open a DOC/DOCX, XLS/XLSX, or PPT/PPTX file. If you have connected more than one account, choose one in the avatar picker. Naro uploads the file to that account’s Google Drive and opens the corresponding editor.

  4. Edit in your browser.

    Make your changes and let Google save them. Keep Naro running and your Mac online so the saved changes can return to the local file. Closing Naro’s window is fine; quitting the app pauses synchronization.

Keep an eye on compatibility.

Files up to 100 MB are supported. Complex Office formatting, macros, and advanced features depend on what Google’s editors support.

Google accounts

The right account
for every file.

Keep work and personal documents with the account you choose.

WP

  1. Add another account.

    Open Naro, go to the account screen, and use the + button. Complete Google sign-in in your browser with the account you want to add. Signing in to an existing account updates its authorization instead of adding a duplicate.

  2. Choose an avatar when opening a file.

    With one account, Naro opens the file directly. With several, a small account picker appears first. Hover over an avatar to see the account’s name and email, then select it.

  3. Cancel without uploading.

    Press Escape or click outside the picker to cancel. Naro does not upload the file until an account has been selected.

  4. Remove an account when you need to.

    Use the remove control beside the account on the account screen. Naro clears its saved sign-in on this Mac and stops syncing that account. Local documents, history, backups, and Google Drive files remain.

Disconnecting is different from revoking access.

To revoke Google’s authorization as well, remove Naro from your Google Account’s third-party connections. See the privacy policy for deletion details.

Saving & sync

Keep your changes
coming home.

Know what gets saved, and what happens when two copies change.

⇄

  1. Give Google time to save.

    While Naro is running and online, it checks linked files for changes. It can download updates only after Google has saved them. Saving time also depends on the network and file size; it is not instantaneous.

  2. Leave Naro running.

    You can close the Naro window and continue working. There is no menu-bar icon; open the app again to see your recent files. If you quit Naro, reopen it to resume synchronization.

  3. Choose a version if both copies changed.

    If the file changes locally and in Google, Naro preserves both versions and asks which one to continue with. Choose the Google version, the local version, or decide later. Naro also creates a backup before overwriting a local document.

  4. Keep the original file link.

    If Google upgrades an older DOC, XLS, or PPT file, Naro saves a modern-format copy alongside the original. Using Google’s separate “Save as Google Docs/Sheets/Slides” conversion creates another document that the original Naro link does not track.

Backups stay on your Mac.

They are stored under ~/Library/Application Support/Naro/Backups/ and are not automatically deleted. Deleting a local document does not delete its Drive copy, and deleting a Drive copy does not delete your local document.
